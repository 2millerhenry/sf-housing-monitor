How do I get an app password?

An app password is a 16-character code your mail provider makes for one program. It is not your normal password, it can only read mail, and you can cancel it later without changing anything else. Find the steps for your provider below.

Gmail gmail.com, googlemail.com

First, check one thing: app passwords do not exist until 2-Step Verification is on. Open myaccount.google.com/security and look for 2-Step Verification. If it says Off, turn it on and finish the phone setup before going on.

  1. Open myaccount.google.com/apppasswords and sign in again if asked.
  2. In App name, type something you will recognise later — Housing Monitor is fine — then press Create.
  3. Google shows 16 letters in four groups. Copy them.abcd efgh ijkl mnop
  4. Paste them into App password at the top of this page and press Connect.

Google will not show you that code again. That is normal, and it costs nothing: if you lose it, come back and make another.

iCloud icloud.com, me.com, mac.com
  1. Open account.apple.com and sign in.
  2. Choose Sign-In and Security.
  3. Choose App-Specific Passwords. If you do not see it, two-factor authentication is off; turn it on first and come back.
  4. Press +, give it a name such as Housing Monitor, then Create.
  5. Copy the 16 characters Apple shows and paste them into App password above.
Yahoo and AOL yahoo.com, aol.com
  1. Open your account security page: login.yahoo.com/account/security for Yahoo, or login.aol.com/account/security for AOL.
  2. Choose Generate app password (sometimes written “Generate and manage app passwords”).
  3. Give it a name such as Housing Monitor and press Generate.
  4. Copy the 16 characters and paste them into App password above.
Fastmail fastmail.com, fastmail.fm
  1. Open Settings › Privacy & Security › Connected apps.
  2. Choose New app password.
  3. For access, pick Mail (IMAP/POP/SMTP). Read-only is enough.
  4. Copy the code Fastmail shows and paste it into App password above.
Outlook.com, Hotmail, Live not supported

Microsoft has switched personal Outlook.com mail to sign-in-only access and no longer issues app passwords that can read mail this way. There is no step here that will work. Your options are to use a different mailbox for housing alerts, or to leave the six free sources running on their own.

Using a different provider?

The five above are recognised from your email address alone. For anything else, ask your provider for its IMAP server (it usually looks like imap.yourprovider.com) and enter all three here.
